Writing Latin verse

One of the aims of VATES is to feature articles on the history of the genre and hints and tips about writing Latin poetry. Here is where to find them.

Download

Classical Latin Poetry: Some Introductory Notes

Notes by Mark Walker on how Latin verse works, for readers who know English poetry but are new to the quantitative verse of the Romans. They begin with what Latin and English poetry have in common and how they differ (stress versus quantity), then cover:

  • rhetorical features of Latin poetry, from alliteration and chiasmus to zeugma
  • recitation and the commonest metrical feet
  • rules for heavy and light syllables, and for the length of vowels
  • word stress, elision, the beat (ictus), caesura and diaeresis
  • the hexameter and the elegiac couplet, with a poetical glossary
  • Chapter 2, ‘Lyrical Latin’: stress-accented Medieval hymns and lyrics, among them O quanta qualia, Veni Creator Spiritus, Dies irae, Stabat Mater and Ave maris stella
  • scanned elegiac and hendecasyllabic epitaphs
  • ‘Verba Modulata’ on Latin song lyrics, and ‘Advice for Beginners’ columns on trochees, vowel length versus syllable quantity, and hexameter endings and the Adonic
